## Iran: talks continue under threat

US President Donald Trump said on Sunday the 8th of November that renewed attacks on Iran were “highly unlikely” if negotiations made progress.[1][2]
The talks concern Iran’s nuclear programme and reopening the Strait of Hormuz, the waterway whose disruption has restricted Gulf energy exports.[1][2]

Iran is seeking sanctions relief, access to frozen assets and war reparations, according to Reuters.[1]
Trump also threatened Iranian bridges and power plants if fighting resumed.[2]
US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said he did not expect an imminent diplomatic breakthrough.[1]
For countries depending on those energy shipments, the negotiations have a very practical importance.

## New Zealand waits for its government

Saturday’s election left Prime Minister Christopher Luxon’s National Party and its partners ACT and New Zealand First provisionally holding 67 of a projected 131 parliamentary seats, with 66 needed for a majority.[3][4]
Around 399,000 special votes remain to be counted, RNZ reports.[4]
These include ballots from overseas and people voting outside their local electorates.[3]

Official results are due on the 27th of November, and Luxon will lead a caretaker government while a governing coalition is formed.[3]
Neither Luxon nor Labour opposition leader Chris Hipkins claimed victory after an election dominated by the economy and living costs.[3]

## Science catch-up: traces in dinosaur bone

A report from Australia’s Curtin University on the 3rd of November describes molecular traces of blood in a 115-million-year-old dinosaur bone from Brazil.[5]
The team found heme, the oxygen-carrying component of haemoglobin, inside structures it identifies as mineralised red blood cells.[5]

That identification is disputed by Mary Schweitzer, a palaeontologist at North Carolina State University, who questions whether the structures have the right shape.[6]
She was more persuaded by the molecular evidence.[6]
The work could help researchers explore dinosaur metabolism and activity levels, offering a different way to study animals usually encountered as bones and teeth.[5][6]

## Switzerland honours a space-science career

Thomas Zurbuchen received the Prix Suisse in Bern on Saturday the 7th of November.[7][8]
The annual award recognises achievement in business, science, sport or culture.[7]
Zurbuchen led the science programme at NASA, the US space agency, from 2016 to 2022.[7][8]

His responsibilities included the James Webb Space Telescope, the first helicopter flight on Mars and DART, the mission that deliberately changed an asteroid’s course.[8]
The award organiser also records that he was the first in his family to attend university.[8]
That is quite a journey from a Swiss village to helping investigate other worlds.

## Senegal’s first Youth Olympic golds

Sixteen-year-old Elisabeth Diatta won Senegal’s first Youth Olympic gold on Saturday, beating Japan’s Reisa Kojima in the women’s 55kg beach-wrestling final in Saly.[9][10]
Maguette Diop followed with gold in the men’s 80kg division, defeating Azerbaijan’s Nihad Suleymanli.[9][10]

Both victories came on home sand at the Dakar Youth Olympic Games.[9][10]
Diatta had beaten the reigning under-17 world champion.[9]
After his win, Diop ran around the ring carrying Senegal’s flag as the crowd celebrated.[10]
