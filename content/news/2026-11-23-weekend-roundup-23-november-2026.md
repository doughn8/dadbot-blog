Five stories from the weekend of the 21st and 22nd of November, starting in Antalya and ending rather further away.

## Türkiye: Climate talks end without a main deal

The UN climate conference closed on Sunday in Antalya, Türkiye, without agreement on its main text.[1][2]
The final draft did not mention coal, oil or gas, or call for countries to phase them out, ABC News reported.[2]
The European Union was among those that objected, while developing countries and their allies also argued that money from richer countries to deal with climate impacts was too little and too slow to arrive.[2]

In a separate decision, countries extended a programme for three years to look at how workers and communities can be supported as high-emissions industries change.[1]

## Trade: A deal that reaches the shoe rack

The EU and Indonesia signed a trade deal in Brussels on Saturday after a decade of negotiations.[3][4]
Indonesia's government says trainers, leather shoes and textiles are among the exports expected to qualify for zero EU import duties once the deal takes effect.[3]
That is the tax charged when goods enter a market.

Indonesia would also phase out duties across most categories of goods it imports from the EU.[4]
The European Parliament still has to consent before the EU can formally conclude the agreement; putting parts of it into effect sooner requires both sides to complete their own procedures.[4]

## Cinema: The Hunger Games draws a worldwide crowd

*The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping* sold an estimated US$225 million in cinema tickets worldwide on its opening weekend.[5][6]
Variety reported US$105 million from international markets, with the film topping charts in markets including Mexico, China and Germany.[5][6]

It's the sixth Hunger Games film, and it takes a familiar character back to his teenage years: Haymitch Abernathy, the mentor played by Woody Harrelson in the original films.[5][6]
The previous prequel opened to about US$101 million worldwide, less than half the new film’s take.[6]

## Formula 1: A finish less than a second apart

Red Bull's Max Verstappen won the Las Vegas Grand Prix, finishing 0.639 seconds ahead of McLaren's Lando Norris.[7][8]
Grandprix.com counted 35 changes of lead.[8]

Mercedes driver George Russell was fighting for a place in the top three when his tyres locked under braking and he hit the barrier late in the race.[7]
His Mercedes team-mate Kimi Antonelli had briefly led, then lost pace towards the end, allowing both McLarens through.[7][8]
Oscar Piastri had started 17th.[8]
He finished third.[7]

## Space: The long way round to Mercury

BepiColombo, a joint mission from the European and Japanese space agencies, entered orbit around Mercury on Saturday, eight years after leaving Earth.[9][10]
It passed Earth once, Venus twice and Mercury six times along the way.[10][11]
The Sun's gravity makes a spacecraft speed up as it falls towards Mercury, so the route used those close passes to help it slow down enough to stay.[10][11]

The mission team confirmed the spacecraft was in orbit, then reported that a software issue had cut short the engine firing that put it there.[10][11]
The two probes, still joined for now, are designed to study Mercury from different orbits.[9][10]
