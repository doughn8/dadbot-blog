Senegal are officially the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations champions again. On the 24th of November 2026, the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS), a tribunal that settles sporting disputes, overturned the decision that had awarded the final to Morocco by forfeit.[3][8]
The official result is back to the one on the scoreboard at the end of extra time in Rabat: Senegal 1, Morocco 0.[5][7]

The match itself took place on the 18th of January 2026. In March, the appeals board of the Confederation of African Football (CAF), which runs the tournament, changed the score to 3–0 in Morocco’s favour.[4][5]
By November, the case in Switzerland turned on whether the referee had the authority to tell both teams to play on.[7][8]

## The final in Rabat

The game was still goalless in second-half stoppage time when Jean-Jacques Ndala blew for a foul on Morocco defender Achraf Hakimi just before Senegal winger Ismaïla Sarr headed the ball into the net, BBC Sport reported.[8]
A few minutes later, Morocco’s Brahim Díaz went down in the Senegal box. Ndala went to the pitchside monitor after the video assistant referee recommended a review, then awarded a penalty.[8]

Senegal coach Pape Thiaw called his players over to the touchline and went down the tunnel, with several players following him in protest at the penalty decision.[8]
BBC Sport puts the time between the award of the penalty and the kick at more than 15 minutes.[8]
When play resumed, Díaz tried to chip the ball down the middle. Édouard Mendy, the Senegal goalkeeper, saved it.[8]

The teams were still level after stoppage time, so the final went to extra time: two additional periods in which to find a winner. Three minutes in, Senegal midfielder Pape Gueye hit a left-footed shot from the edge of the area. It was the only goal of the night and gave Senegal their second Africa Cup of Nations title.[5][8]
Morocco had been playing in front of a home crowd for a trophy they had waited more than five decades to win again.[5]

## How the result became a forfeit

The two boards were answering a different question from the one facing the referee at the monitor. The video review was about contact in the penalty area. Morocco’s protest concerned what happened after the penalty was given: whether leaving the pitch made Senegal liable to lose the final under the tournament rules.[4][8]

Morocco’s football federation challenged the result under the tournament’s rules on withdrawal. CAF’s disciplinary board rejected the protest, and its decision of the 11th of February left the result in place.[3][4][8]
The federation took the case to CAF’s appeals board, which came to a different conclusion on the 17th of March.[4]

Two provisions of the tournament rules were at the centre of the dispute. Article 82 deals with teams that refuse to play or leave the ground before the end without the referee’s permission. Article 84 is the provision the appeals board used to record the match as a 3–0 forfeit.[4][8]
The board found that Senegal’s conduct fell under both articles and upheld Morocco’s protest.[4]

Senegal’s federation then appealed to CAS. The three-person panel heard the case behind closed doors in Lausanne, Switzerland, on the 8th of October 2026. Its November decision was unanimous.[8]

## Why the restart mattered

Morocco’s federation argued that the withdrawal rules required the referee to end the match early. It said his decision to resume play was arbitrary and that the rules on discipline made this more than an ordinary on-pitch judgment.[7][8]
Senegal’s federation argued that the restart was precisely the kind of call that a referee is there to make, and that an appeals body should leave that decision in place.[8]

Both teams came back onto the pitch and indicated they were ready to play. Ndala blew his whistle to resume the final, a decision the panel said was his to make.[7][8]
The arbitrators treated it as a decision about what was happening on the pitch and found that the referee had acted within his authority. Sports lawyers call this the *field of play* doctrine. In practice, it means the tribunal normally leaves refereeing decisions alone unless there is evidence the rules were applied arbitrarily or in bad faith.[8]

The panel read the two withdrawal articles as rules for a match the referee has called off permanently. In this case, he restarted it and the teams played to the end.[7][8]
CAF had separately disciplined Senegal’s federation, coach Pape Thiaw and several players. The National names Iliman Ndiaye and Ismaïla Sarr among the players sanctioned in those proceedings.[7]

## Morocco says it will challenge the ruling

CAF says it respects the award and will take the necessary steps to implement it.[3]
Senegal’s federation welcomed the ruling, and its president, Abdoulaye Fall, said the federation was ready to put the dispute behind it.[7][8]
Morocco’s federation said it was surprised by the decision and would pursue legal avenues to challenge it.[7][8]

Morocco’s federation alleges there was contact between Fall and Ulrich Haas, one of the three arbitrators, before the ruling. Senegal’s federation denied that a call took place, and CAS said Haas denied direct contact with any party to the case.[7][8]
The route for a challenge is the Swiss Federal Supreme Court, Switzerland’s highest court, which can set an arbitration decision aside.[7][8]

## A rule for future walk-offs

The January walk-off has already had a consequence beyond this final, BBC Sport reports.[8]
On the 28th of April 2026, the International Football Association Board (IFAB), the body that writes football’s laws, approved a change for future games. Where a competition chooses to use it, a referee can show a red card to a player who walks off in protest at a decision, or to a team official who urges players to do so.[9]

The IFAB announced the measure for the 2026 World Cup. Its announcement also said that a team responsible for a match being abandoned would in principle forfeit the game.[9]
For the players, that makes walking off a potential sending-off offence even if the rest of the match goes ahead.[9]
