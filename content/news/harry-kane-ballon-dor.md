Harry Kane has won the 2026 men’s Ballon d’Or, becoming the first Englishman to take the award since Michael Owen in 2001.[1][2]
The Bayern Munich striker and England captain received it at the London Palladium on the 26th of October, after a season in which he scored 73 goals for club and country.[1][3]

That is the headline, and it is a good one. The English wait gives the evening its historical weight, but Kane’s season deserves more than a quick mention before a trip down memory lane. The goals, the trophies and the route he took to get here are the story.

## What happened

Kane finished ahead of Kylian Mbappé, the Real Madrid and France striker, and Lamine Yamal, Barcelona’s Spain winger.[2][3]
The Ballon d’Or is an individual football award presented by French magazine France Football.[2]

London was hosting the ceremony for the first time, in recognition of the inaugural winner, England’s Stanley Matthews, 70 years earlier.[3]
An English winner at that particular ceremony made for a rather neat ending.

There was a less tidy build-up: Sky reported that the award’s website briefly published separate stories naming Kane, Mbappé and Yamal as the winner, before removing them.[2]
The announced result was Kane’s win.[1][3]
That is the result to remember, rather than the website’s enthusiastic attempt to cover every possibility.

## The season behind the award

England Football, the Football Association’s official site, records 73 goals in 65 games: 61 for Bayern and 12 for England.[1]

Bayern won Germany’s league, the Bundesliga, its main knockout cup, the DFB-Pokal, and the German Supercup, named after Franz Beckenbauer.[1]
The Associated Press (AP) news agency reports that Kane scored a hat-trick (three goals in one match) against Stuttgart in the cup final, while Bayern also reached the Champions League semi-finals.[3]
The Champions League brings together leading clubs from across Europe.

Kane’s England campaign also mattered. AP reports that he scored six times at the World Cup, passed Gary Lineker as England’s leading scorer in the tournament’s history, and captained the team to third place.[3]
England lost to Argentina in the semi-finals before beating France in the third-place match.[3]

Taken together, that is a substantial sporting case: goals across club and international football, domestic silverware and a deep World Cup run. The award recognises an individual, but the season behind it was played in several different shirts and competitions.

## What we know so far

- Kane also received the Gerd Müller Trophy, the ceremony’s separate award for men’s striker of the year.[1]
- He is the fifth Englishman to win the men’s Ballon d’Or; Kevin Keegan won it twice.[4]
- The previous winner, Owen, received the award in 2001 after a year of success with Liverpool and England.[2][4]

## Why England’s wait stands out

The English winners’ list is short enough to read without settling in for the evening. Before Kane, it contained four names.[4]

**Stanley Matthews, 1956.** The Blackpool winger won the first award at 41, finishing ahead of Real Madrid’s Alfredo Di Stéfano.[4]
His age alone is a useful reminder that there is no single timetable for an outstanding football career.

**Bobby Charlton, 1966.** The England midfielder won in the year his country lifted the World Cup, after scoring three goals during the tournament.[4]

**Kevin Keegan, 1978 and 1979.** He won both awards while playing for Hamburg, with the second following a Bundesliga-winning season.[4]

**Michael Owen, 2001.** The Liverpool forward helped his club win the FA Cup, League Cup and UEFA Cup, scoring twice late in the FA Cup final against Arsenal.[4]
He also scored a hat-trick in England’s 5–1 win over Germany in Munich that year.[4]

Now Kane adds a new name. A quarter of a century separates his win from Owen’s, which explains the historical interest in this particular result.

## A long way from the loan spells

Kane used his speech to describe a route that had not gone straight to the top, including loan moves and having to fight for his place.[2]
He also credited the teammates who created chances and drew defenders away, naming players from Bayern and England.[2]

That is worth keeping in the picture. A goals total makes a wonderfully simple headline. It does not show every pass that made the finish possible, or every period when progress was less obvious.

AP described crowds waiting outside the Palladium and chanting Kane’s name after the ceremony.[3]
Kane’s own celebration had a practical limit: Bayern had a cup game on Wednesday, and he was flying back for training on Tuesday.[3]
Even a Ballon d’Or does not get the next fixture moved.
