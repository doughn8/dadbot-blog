Russell Brand has been sentenced to 10 years and six months in prison for raping one woman and sexually assaulting three others.[1]
The sentence was imposed at Southwark Crown Court in London on the 9th of November 2026, four days after the jury returned its verdicts.[1][2]

The British comedian, actor and former television presenter, 51, was convicted of offences committed between 2004 and 2009.[2][4]
At the sentencing hearing, the women described lasting effects on their health, work and ability to trust people.[3][5]

## What the jury decided

After a four-week trial, the jury found Brand guilty of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault.[2]
A count is an individual criminal charge: in this case, the four convictions concern four women.[1][2]
The jury also found him not guilty of two further rape charges and one charge of attempted indecent assault.[4]

The rape conviction concerned an attack in 2009, while the sexual assault convictions concerned incidents in 2004, 2004 or 2005, and 2009.[4]
Brand had denied every charge and maintained that his sexual encounters were consensual.[4]
His defence argued that he had not acted unlawfully.[4]

The trial heard allegations from six women covering the period from 1999 to 2009, according to the BBC's court reporting.[4]
The judge's sentencing remarks address the four offences for which the jury convicted him, with the punishment set out separately for each.[3]

## How the sentence works

Mr Justice Joel Bennathan, the judge who sentenced Brand, imposed the following prison terms:[3]

- **Rape:** nine years.
- **Sexual assault, count three:** nine months.
- **Sexual assault, count five:** 18 months.
- **Sexual assault, count seven:** eight months.[3]

The three sexual assault sentences run concurrently, meaning they are served at the same time.[3]
The longest of those terms is 18 months, and those 18 months run consecutively to the nine-year rape sentence, meaning one follows the other.[3]
Together, they make 10 years and six months.[3]

The judge explained that he had to consider the overall punishment when sentencing several offences.[3]
This is called totality: the combined sentence must be just and proportionate.[3]
He reduced the sentence for one sexual assault from two years to 18 months as part of that assessment.[3]

He also considered Brand's work helping people with addictions, the time since the offences and the absence of significant previous convictions.[3]
The judge said it would be wrong to disregard the mitigation altogether and reduced the rape sentence by one year.[3]
Mitigation means circumstances put forward to reduce the punishment.[3]

Under the terms set out in the remarks, Brand will serve two thirds of the overall sentence in custody before release on licence.[3]
That is seven years, with the days already spent in custody counting towards the sentence.[3]
Release on licence means he remains subject to conditions and can be returned to prison if he breaches them.[3]
He will also be subject indefinitely to notification requirements under the Sexual Offences Act 2003, commonly described as being on the sex offenders register.[3][5]

## The impact described in court

The women's accounts at sentencing described consequences that continued long after the attacks.[3]
The woman Brand raped spoke of struggling to eat and sleep, taking time away from work and continuing to find it difficult to trust people and form friendships.[3]
The judge recorded that she had abandoned the career she had begun.[3]

One woman who was sexually assaulted described feeling vulnerable when going out and becoming distressed when she saw Brand in newspapers or on television.[3]
Another described being startled by his voice on television and the damage the assault had done to her relationships with male colleagues.[3]
For the woman assaulted while working on a television project, the experience affected her confidence and left her feeling unsafe in her workplace.[3][5]

The judge also addressed the people around Brand during two of the assaults, describing an entourage that had indulged his behaviour.[3]
In one account he accepted, people present watched an assault without intervening, and someone laughed afterwards.[3]
He considered the added humiliation of an audience when assessing the seriousness of the offending.[3]

All the women have lifelong anonymity.[1]
The judge said that protection covers both naming them and publishing enough details for their identities to be worked out.[3]

## How the case reached court

The public reporting began in September 2023, when the Times, the Sunday Times and Channel 4's Dispatches programme published allegations about Brand.[4]
He strongly denied the allegations at the time.[4]
The CPS said the television programme prompted women to come forward independently and report offences to police.[2]

The offences that led to these convictions had not been reported to police when they happened, according to the CPS, the body that brought the prosecution.[2]
Its account of building the case describes prosecutors bringing together the women's testimony and evidence from other witnesses to show the jury what had happened.[2]
The passage of time had limited the evidence available, it said.[2]

The judge described Brand's growing status as having protected him until the reporting in 2023.[3]
His remarks place the offences within the years when Brand's fame was increasing and record how the women continued to encounter his public image afterwards.[3]

The jury delivered its verdicts on the 5th of November after the four-week trial.[2]
Brand was taken into custody, with sentencing following on the 9th of November.[1][4]
At that hearing, the judge heard the women's accounts of the impact before imposing the prison terms.[3]

## What happens next

Police work is continuing.[6]
In reporting published after sentencing, the BBC said several people had contacted the Metropolitan Police during and after the trial to provide information or report potential criminal allegations.[6]
Detectives were assessing that information to establish what it concerned and whether it could amount to a criminal offence.[6]

The force is also investigating rape allegations from two further women, and Brand has been interviewed under caution in connection with those allegations, the BBC reported.[6]
Those enquiries remain ongoing.[6]
