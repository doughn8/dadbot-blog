A rocket returning home, a late winner and a sky full of balloons: here are five stories for your Monday catch-up. Four come from the weekend, with a Friday technology extra from Oxford.

## Texas: a rocket comes back to its tower

SpaceX caught its Starship upper stage at its launch tower for the first time on Saturday, the 14th of November, after roughly 22.5 hours away.[1][2]
This is the spacecraft that rides above the Super Heavy booster, continues into space and, on this flight, released 26 Starlink satellites.[1]

The booster diverted offshore after an early end to its return burn and was destroyed; the spacecraft returned to the Texas launch site and settled into the tower's catching arms.[1]
For SpaceX's goal of a reusable launch system, bringing that second part home is a substantial step.[2]

## India: a thousand drums for Wangala

The Hundred Drums Wangala Festival went considerably beyond its name on Saturday: NorthEast Now reports 1,000 traditional drummers and 3,000 dancers performing near Tura, in India's northeastern state of Meghalaya.[3]
It was the golden-jubilee celebration of a festival showcasing Garo culture.[3]

The Sentinel reports that a Guinness World Records adjudicator announced the record for the largest Wangala dance at the venue.[4]
The organised festival began in 1976 with 20 drums, according to the same report, before growing to 100 and then this year's thousand.[4]
More than 300 festival stalls displayed Garo food, handloom products, handicrafts and traditional jewellery, with folk performances, indigenous games and competitions alongside the drumming.[3]

## Singapore: Japan leave it late against Brazil

Japan beat Brazil 3–2 in Singapore on Saturday, with substitute Tatsuhiro Sakamoto scoring the winner in added time.[5][6]
Japan had led 2–0 before Brazil pulled level, leaving the match set up for a late finish.[5][6]

The winning move involved several Japanese substitutes, ending with Sakamoto reacting to a loose ball and firing home.[5]
Brazil's bench protested over a possible foul in the build-up; coach Carlo Ancelotti and the already-substituted Raphinha were sent off afterwards.[6]

## Friday catch-up: an ancient shrine gets its colours back

Oxford's Ashmolean Museum introduced a colourful projection onto the Shrine of King Taharqa on Friday, the 13th of November.[7][11]
The 2,700-year-old building originally stood in northern Sudan; researchers studied surviving pigment traces to reconstruct its painted decoration.[7]

Visitors can now see blues, reds and yellows projected onto the sandstone carvings, with two short animated films explaining the shrine's history and imagery.[7][11]
Clay Interactive, which made the installation, describes a carved vulture taking flight in one sequence.[11]
The light leaves the original stonework untouched and lets the reconstruction change as research develops.[11]

## Mexico: balloons before breakfast

Hundreds of hot-air balloons rose over León on Sunday morning during the city's International Balloon Festival, local newspaper AM reports.[9]
Families arrived before sunrise with blankets, jackets and hot drinks, then watched the balloons take shape and lift above the El Palote reservoir.[9]

The festival runs from the 13th to the 16th of November at the city's Metropolitan Park.[10]
Sunday also brought its first morning concert, with Latin pop group Santos Bravos taking the stage at 8am.[9]
Balloons overhead, hot drinks in hand and a concert before breakfast: a fairly ambitious start to a Sunday.
