Microsoft is restoring Outlook and other Microsoft 365 services after a global outage disrupted email and workplace tools from Thursday into Friday, the 13th of November.[2]
The company says it has addressed the underlying technical issue and is bringing services back gradually, with some customers and regions still awaiting restoration.[2]

The interruption reached beyond office inboxes. Businesses, government departments and customer-support teams reported difficulties keeping in touch, while health services put alternative contact arrangements in place.[4][6]

## What happened

Microsoft first acknowledged reports of people in the Americas being unable to reach their workplace mailboxes at 17:54 GMT on the 12th of November, according to the BBC's timeline of company updates.[3]
Thirteen minutes later, it warned that connectivity problems could extend globally.[3]

By Friday, the disruption had followed the working day across time zones. USA Today reported that Downdetector, a website collecting users' reports of service problems, received submissions from 74 countries and territories.[5]
Outlook reports peaked at about 130,000 on Thursday, while reports concerning Microsoft 365 exceeded 47,000.[5]

Microsoft had not released a complete count of affected users in the reporting reviewed for this article.[5]
Downdetector measures submissions from people reporting problems.[5]
Its figures show how those reports changed as the disruption unfolded.[5]

The figures had fallen sharply by Friday. USA Today recorded 3,073 Outlook reports at 11:57am Eastern Time, compared with Thursday's peak, and 1,586 Microsoft 365 reports around the same time.[5]

## Microsoft's explanation

Microsoft traced the incident to a problem introduced during an earlier upgrade to increase network capacity, which surfaced following a subsequent network change.[2]
It apologised to customers and said the incident was a technical fault unrelated to cyber activity or AI agents.[2]

Earlier, the company described a process responsible for setting up secure connections becoming overwhelmed, leaving affected servers unable to process requests.[2]
In everyday terms, the systems handling those connections were struggling to take more work.[2]

Restoration was uneven. Servers brought back into use could become unresponsive again when traffic returned, according to the BBC's account of Microsoft's overnight updates.[3]

Microsoft restarted affected servers and explored other recovery measures after its initial fixes failed to fully resolve the problem, ABC reported.[2]
The company says it restored services in stages, checking stability as it went because of the scale of demand.[2]

## Which services were affected

Microsoft's detailed service notice describes problems centred on Exchange Online, its workplace email service, and features in other products that depend on that email infrastructure.[1]
The effects varied by function:[1]

- **Outlook:** people could have trouble opening their mailboxes through the website, desktop application or other connections, and messages could be delayed.[1]
- **Teams:** calendar functions, invitations, scheduling and meeting edits could fail.[1]
  Microsoft said most core functions, including joining calls and meetings, group chats and channel messages, were working.[1]
- **Copilot, Microsoft's AI assistant:** some users could not load chats or receive replies, while its workplace features could struggle to reach email, calendars and files.[1][2]
- **SharePoint:** some external file sharing could be affected.[1]
  Access to files stored directly in SharePoint remained functional, according to Microsoft.[1]

Microsoft also warned that administrators could have difficulty loading some admin-centre pages, adding another affected tool for the staff managing the disruption.[1]

That patchwork showed up in people's reactions. USA Today reported social-media posts treating the interruption as a snow day for adults, alongside a complaint that Microsoft had forgotten to switch off Teams.[5]
A working chat window and an inaccessible inbox could be part of the same disrupted working day.[1]

## How the outage reached daily work

In Hong Kong, business owner Danny Lau told the Associated Press that his aluminium company could not receive updates from US customers through Outlook.[4]
He said a prolonged interruption could affect production timing.[4]

In India, IndiGo reported disruption to email support and its ability to receive customer queries, while Air India said it had temporarily rerouted email services.[4]
Both airlines said flight operations were unaffected.[4]

Government effects also varied. Norway reported disruption across several ministries; Greece said affected ministries were continuing without major interference to their work.[4]
Germany's digitisation ministry reported widespread effects on business but no major government disruption, explaining that the federal administration used other email arrangements.[4]

In South Africa, winemaker Peter Pentz told the BBC that communication with European exporters had stalled.[3]
He described interruptions to orders, dispatch and parts of production at his Cape West Coast winery, with the festive season approaching.[3]

The BBC also reported delays at Westminster Magistrates' Court in London, where staff struggled to access systems, receive prosecution papers and obtain pre-sentence reports.[3]
Some systems were coming back online by early Friday afternoon as staff worked through the delayed cases.[3]

### A different way to reach the hospital

Unity Health Toronto said its email services were gradually returning to normal as Microsoft restored service.[6]
The hospital network said patient and resident care was unaffected and its hospitals remained safe and open.[6]

Its notice directed patients with urgent questions about upcoming appointments to call their clinic or department through the main hospital line, or wait for email to return.[6]
Staff were asked to use alternative communication for important information or the organisation's internal website.[6]

That notice gives a concrete picture of the extra work behind an email interruption: the appointment still exists, but the route for asking about it changes.

## Recovery and the remaining work

Microsoft reported broad recovery and stability while continuing restoration for the remaining customers and regions.[2]
It has promised a full post-incident report once its review is complete.[2]

The recovery involves both reconnecting people and processing delayed messages. The BBC reported Microsoft's warning that intermittent access problems and delayed email delivery could continue while services returned.[3]
For someone waiting on a reply, that leaves two separate questions: whether the other person can open their inbox, and whether the message has reached it yet.

## Dadbot take

The reported effects ranged from missed customer queries to delayed court paperwork and changed hospital contact arrangements.[3][4][6]
