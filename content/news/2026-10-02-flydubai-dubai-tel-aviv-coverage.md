A Flydubai flight from Dubai to Tel Aviv diverted to Tabuk in Saudi Arabia on the 30th of September after an altercation in the cockpit. The airline said travelling crew secured the aircraft and landed it safely.[3]
Both pilots were injured, according to Saudi preliminary findings reported by The National.[2]

The accounts now include an assault finding, Israeli officials’ allegations of an attempt to crash the plane, and a UAE investigation examining possible terrorist intent. Those are different parts of the record, not interchangeable conclusions.[2][19][25]
This article compares named reports checked on the 2nd of October, without assigning motives to the incident or to the outlets covering it.

## What happened

Flight FZ1073 was travelling from Dubai International Airport to Ben Gurion International Airport, which serves Tel Aviv. Gulf News carried Flydubai’s statement describing an altercation on the flight deck — the cockpit — followed by intervention from on-duty crew travelling on the flight.[3]

On the 1st of October, the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs announced that Attorney-General Hamad Al Shamsi had ordered a prosecution investigation. Its statement said the aircraft was registered in the UAE and that UAE judicial authorities had jurisdiction, including over crimes committed aboard it outside UAE territory.[25]

Reuters subsequently reported that the co-pilot had been transferred to the UAE and that Israeli officials were expected to question him.[19]

## Why this matters

The immediate consequences were injuries, emergency intervention and a diverted journey. The National reported that Saudi Arabia’s Interior Ministry said the co-pilot assaulted the captain and that both sustained injuries. It also reported that both pilots left for Abu Dhabi after their conditions improved.[2]

ABC’s reconstruction reports that an additional Flydubai crew took control and diverted the plane, attributing that account to the airline. Its passenger reporting describes medical assistance and supplies after landing in Saudi Arabia.[6]

## What we know so far

- **The flight landed safely in Tabuk.** Flydubai said the aircraft was secured by crew travelling aboard it.[3]
- **Saudi preliminary findings describe an assault.** The National attributes that finding to the Saudi Interior Ministry.[2]
- **The UAE inquiry includes possible terrorism.** The ministry’s announcement also includes possible prior planning or direction among the questions being examined.[25]
- **Israeli participation was reported as forthcoming.** Reuters quoted an Israeli government official saying investigators would join the UAE-led inquiry. It also said the UAE foreign ministry had not immediately answered its request for comment about questioning the co-pilot.[19]

## What is still unclear

The checked UAE statement does not announce a finding on motive. Reuters’ report of the 2nd of October says that a motive had not been established, while reporting Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s assertions about the co-pilot’s ideology and intent.[25][19]

The full cockpit sequence is also not independently established by the reports reviewed here. The Jerusalem Post explicitly says it had not independently established the precise sequence or duration of an interval described by one passenger, during which the cockpit was reportedly empty.[18]

## Israeli coverage

The Jerusalem Post reports a dispute about pilot eligibility on Israel-bound flights. Unnamed sources familiar with the investigation rejected a claim that the bilateral aviation agreement required pilots to be nationals of countries with diplomatic ties to Israel; an earlier Transportation Ministry source had described that requirement.[18]

The report distinguishes the agreement from separate operating or security arrangements, leaving unresolved whether the co-pilot’s assignment breached the applicable conditions.[18]

## UAE and Gulf coverage

Gulf News leads with Flydubai’s account of a cockpit altercation and safe landing, while The National leads with the UAE inquiry.[3][2]
The National also reports that claims about the co-pilot’s Omani nationality had not been confirmed by Oman, UAE authorities or the airline.[2]

Al Jazeera includes Israeli opposition leader Yair Lapid’s criticism of attempts to link the incident to earlier security warnings — a political response, not an investigation finding.[12]

## International coverage

Reuters, syndicated by The Jerusalem Post, focuses on expected Israeli participation in the investigation.[19]
ABC Australia reconstructs the flight through tracking data, a passenger’s account and officials’ statements, attributing cockpit allegations to Netanyahu.[6]

## Where the reports overlap

Gulf News, Al Jazeera and ABC all report intervention by additional crew and a safe diversion to Tabuk.[3][12][6]

## Where they differ

The descent figures describe different intervals: Al Jazeera reports almost 14,000 feet lost in less than 30 seconds; ABC records more than 20,000 feet across several minutes, with drops and recoveries.[12][6]
