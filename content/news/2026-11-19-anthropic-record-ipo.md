Anthropic, the company behind the Claude AI models, has priced an $81.3 billion initial public offering, the largest by initial proceeds on record.[1][2]
Its shares were priced at $83 on the 18th of November 2026, ahead of a scheduled debut on the Nasdaq stock exchange on the 19th under the symbol ANT.[1]

The offering puts a roughly $1.5 trillion fully diluted value on a business founded in 2021.[2][3]
Behind those enormous numbers is a fairly straightforward story: investors want a share of a fast-growing AI company, and the company has a very large computing bill.[2][4]

## What the record covers

An initial public offering, usually shortened to IPO, is a company’s first public sale of shares, giving investors a way to buy a stake in the business.[1][5]
Anthropic’s offering consists of 980 million shares at $83 each, producing $81.34 billion in total before fees.[1]

That is substantially larger than the original plan to offer 825 million shares at between $70 and $78.[3][4]
Both the price and the number of shares increased as demand exceeded the shares available, NPR reports.[4]

The comparison with SpaceX, Elon Musk’s space company, is between the initial offerings: SpaceX raised $75 billion in June, then reached $85.7 billion after the banks handling its sale exercised an option to buy additional shares.[3]
Anthropic has granted its own banks a 30-day option for up to 147 million extra shares.[1]
That gives its offering room to grow further, depending on whether the option is used.[1]

## Where the money goes

Most of the shares come from Anthropic itself: the company is selling 956,351,354 new shares, generating about $79.4 billion before fees.[1]
Existing shareholders are selling the remaining 23,648,646 shares for roughly $2 billion.[1]
Those proceeds go to the sellers; Anthropic’s announcement says the company receives none of that portion.[1]

Alongside the public offering, Anthropic is raising another $26 billion through a private placement, Reuters reports, taking the combined transaction to about $107.3 billion.[2]
A private placement sells shares to an agreed group of investors alongside the public sale.[2]
CNBC identifies Google, Microsoft, Nvidia and Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) among the technology companies participating in that investment.[3]

These companies also have business relationships with Anthropic, which buys computing systems from major technology suppliers including Google, Amazon, Nvidia and AMD.[3]

## What a $1.5 trillion valuation means

The valuation describes the price attached to ownership of the whole company, using the IPO share price as the starting point.[2]
Reuters puts Anthropic’s value at about $1.1 trillion on a non-diluted basis and roughly $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis.[2]

The larger figure includes securities such as stock options and restricted stock, accounting for additional ownership interests alongside the ordinary shares.[2]

An everyday illustration helps: selling a small slice of a business establishes a price for that slice, which can then be used to estimate what the whole business is worth. The cash raised comes from the slice sold. The valuation describes the whole pie.

## The business behind Claude

Chief executive Dario Amodei and fellow researchers founded Anthropic in 2021 after leaving OpenAI, CNBC reports.[3]
The company develops the Claude family of AI models and primarily sells its products and services to other businesses.[3]

Claude Code, its coding agent used by software developers, has been an important driver of that growth, according to Reuters.[2]
This places Anthropic in the competition to supply AI tools for everyday business work, alongside rivals including OpenAI and Google.[3]

Its customer figures show how much that business has expanded. As of the 30th of September, Anthropic reported 11,900 customers generating at least $100,000 each in revenue over the previous 12 months, compared with more than 1,500 at the end of 2025.[2]
Those are substantial business accounts, giving the company a growing base of customers buying its services at scale.[2]

The attraction for investors is visible in that growth: a company selling more services to more large customers has a clear commercial story to put beside its technology. The next part of the story is the cost of delivering them.

## The computing bill

Reuters reports that Anthropic generated $16.96 billion in revenue in the third quarter, up 47% from the previous quarter, while recording an operating loss of $567 million.[2]
The company’s own financial highlights round the revenue figure to $17 billion and report a net loss of $23.3 billion.[5]

Anthropic says that net loss includes a $21.9 billion non-cash charge arising from changes in the value of convertible notes, derivative liabilities and other financing instruments.[5]
These are financing arrangements whose accounting values change as the company’s valuation changes.[5]
That accounts for most of the reported net loss.[5]

Computing is a sizeable expense in its own right. Anthropic spent nearly $9 billion on computing capacity and infrastructure in the first half of 2026, followed by another $5 billion in computing costs in the third quarter, Reuters reports.[2]
It has also committed to nearly $900 billion in computing spending with infrastructure partners including Amazon and Google.[2]

The scale of those commitments helps explain the appetite for fresh funding. Growing revenue, buying computing capacity and raising capital are closely connected parts of this business. The public listing will make Anthropic’s financial results a regular reference point for the economics of advanced AI, as investors assess how sales and costs develop together.[2]

## OpenAI could follow

CNBC reports that OpenAI has confidentially filed for an IPO and has said a debut is likely in 2027.[3]

For now, Anthropic is the first major developer of the most advanced AI models to take this step, according to Reuters.[2]
Its results will provide an early public-market comparison for another large AI business considering a listing.[2][3]

## Dadbot take

The money now going into Anthropic connects three parts of the AI story: customers buying its tools, investors funding the business and suppliers providing computing capacity.[1][2][3]
The listing brings those relationships into a single, closely watched public company. Its sales and computing costs will give readers something concrete to follow beyond the size of the debut.[2]
